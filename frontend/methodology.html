03 / THE METHOD

How the calculation works.

A weighted index, not a probability

Risk = 0.35 × rainfall score + 0.30 × soil score + 0.20 × antecedent rainfall score + 0.15 × anomaly score. All input scores are clipped to 0–100.

  • Rainfall: 7-day total ÷ its 98th percentile × 100.
  • Soil: NASA root-zone wetness × 100.
  • Antecedent rainfall: API ÷ its 98th percentile × 100. API today = rainfall today + 0.85 × API yesterday.
  • Anomaly: (rainfall anomaly + 20 mm) ÷ 40 mm × 100. The anomaly subtracts the calendar-month mean in the selected record.

The percentile denominators have a 1 mm floor. Low: ≤40; moderate: >40–60; high: >60–75; critical: >75. For event comparison, a daily index ≥75 counts as reaching the evaluation threshold. Scores depend on the chosen reference period.

Comparing with official flood records

Official event date ranges appear in amber on the risk chart. A record is marked “Threshold reached” if at least one daily index during its documented date range reaches 75. “Below threshold” means the complete event range has data but never reaches 75. Missing days produce “Insufficient data” unless an available day already confirms the threshold was reached.

The current curated register is incomplete. A threshold crossing without a registered flood is not automatically a false positive. A date match is evidence of alignment, not proof that the model is accurate. An accuracy assessment requires a comprehensive event register, verified non-event days and independent validation. No time tolerance or advance-warning window is applied.

Estimated flood and flash-flood signals on the chart

These markers are exploratory hypotheses, separate from the official event register. They do not establish that a flood occurred or identify its hydrological type. Each qualifying day is marked separately; adjacent markers are not a count of distinct events.

  • Yellow diamond — Possible flood: daily risk index ≥75, daily rainfall greater than zero, and rainfall below the wet-day 95th percentile in the selected record.
  • Orange triangle — Possible flash flood: daily risk index ≥75 and daily rainfall at or above that wet-day 95th percentile. This extreme-rain proxy is a project heuristic, not a scientifically validated classifier.

The percentile is calculated using positive-rainfall days only and changes with the selected period. Short records provide unstable thresholds. Missing rainfall or index values produce no marker. A high index on a rain-free day remains visible on the risk line but does not receive an estimated-event marker.

Flash flooding is defined by rapid water rise after the triggering event. Daily precipitation totals cannot determine rainfall intensity within hours or runoff response. Hourly data, terrain and hydrological observations are required to investigate that distinction. A gradual or river flood can also occur during extreme rainfall; these two marker categories are only visual hypotheses.

Mann–Kendall and OLS answer different questions

Mann–Kendall tests whether a historical series has a monotonic trend. Its own p-value, evaluated at α = 0.05, determines the significance shown here. At least eight observations are required by the implementation. OLS estimates the rate of change per year and reports a separate p-value. Neither test contributes to the daily index.

The index is tested using monthly means. Its trend is not seasonally adjusted, and the basic Mann–Kendall implementation does not correct for autocorrelation. Seasonal cycles and short records can distort conclusions. The deseasonalized rainfall series subtracts calendar-month means within the selected record. Partial months and years may affect aggregation. These limitations matter when interpreting statistical significance.

NASA data and spatial limitations

Daily data come from NASA POWER, primarily using MERRA-2: PRECTOTCORR (rainfall), GWETTOP (surface soil wetness) and GWETROOT (root-zone wetness). The model samples a gridded point, not an entire watershed. Sub-daily storms, river flow, drainage and local terrain are not represented by this index.

NASA wetness is a model wetness indicator; displaying it as a percentage does not mean it is a direct measurement of volumetric water content. Missing source values are not dry conditions. The index is exploratory and is not a validated operational flood forecast.
